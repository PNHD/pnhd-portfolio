WHY THIS EXISTS

The $1 idea isn’t new.
The measurement is.

There are many “give me a dollar” pages. Most stop at the gimmick. This one treats the ask as a documented distribution experiment: what channels move it, where does it fail, and how many strangers actually say yes?

The useful result is the funnel: ask → visit → checkout → confirmed $1 → referral.

01

Why exactly $1?

It keeps the requested action small, concrete and comparable. Tips above $1 are reported separately from the primary result.

02

What happens to the money?

It becomes my money. Fees come out first; some of what remains may pay for the domain, email infrastructure, distribution tests and documentation.

03

What do you get?

No product, ownership, return, raffle entry or private perk. The public output is the documented experiment and its aggregate results.

PROTOCOL v0.1 · LOCKED SEP 03, 2026

What exactly gets measured?

PRIMARY

Exact-$1 conversion

Confirmed one-time Ko-fi tips of exactly $1.00 USD divided by best-effort first-seen browser visitors. Clearing storage, private browsing or switching devices can create another first-seen visit, so this is not claimed as perfect person-level deduplication.

DISTRIBUTION

Reach ≠ visitors

Impressions, delivered messages, visits, checkout clicks and confirmed payments are separate stages. Source attribution uses explicit source tags first, then the referrer hostname when available; privacy settings can remove referrer data.

EXCLUSIONS

Keep the baseline clean

Creator tests, refunds, subscriptions, other currencies and tips above the requested amount are separated from the primary result.

PRIVACY

Aggregate results only

Supporter names, emails and payment details are not part of the public dataset.

BASELINE RULE

The first controlled cohort runs without a public supporter counter. A later A/B test may compare counter vs no-counter as a separate social-proof experiment.

Technical diagnostics
Not checked.

FAQ

Everything awkward, answered directly.

Is this a charity?

No. It is a personal public internet experiment.

Why would I give a stranger money for nothing?

You may decide not to. That is part of the experiment. The premise intentionally removes any promised exchange.

Why can’t I see how many people already paid?

Visible progress can influence behavior. The baseline cohort deliberately hides that signal.

Will my name or email be published?

No. Public reporting is aggregate.

Was AI used to build this?

Yes. AI tools helped with research, coding and drafting. Project decisions and submissions are made by the creator. Payments, fees and reported results must come from real events; supporter identities are not published and no results are fabricated.
